Frequently Asked Questions & Project Overview

Key information about our privacy-first, 100% offline security camera mesh architecture.

What is the Linux-First Offline Security Camera Hub? ↓

It is a 100% open-source (AGPLv3/MIT), privacy-focused Network Video Recorder (NVR) and camera mesh system. It is engineered to turn old hardware—such as Android smartphones, Linux laptops, USB webcams, Raspberry Pis, 1U/2U server racks, and flashed Wi-Fi routers—into a unified, zero-cloud security monitoring network.

How does repurposing old phones and laptops save money? ↓

Instead of buying expensive proprietary IP cameras with forced cloud subscriptions, our native Android daemons and Linux V4L2 drivers transform drawer phones and old laptops into high-definition camera nodes. Laptops can also act as Ethernet DHCP gateways and NVR hubs, completely eliminating subscription costs.

Does this system require an internet connection or cloud servers? ↓

No. The core system operates 100% offline over local Wi-Fi and Ethernet networks. Video streams, AI object detection (YOLOv8/ONNX), clip indexing, and motion triggers run locally on your hardware. There are zero cloud account requirements, zero telemetry, and zero mandatory external connections.

What role do custom flashed OpenWrt routers play? ↓

Older routers flashed with OpenWrt or DD-WRT firmware become dedicated system network nodes. They host a hidden encrypted SSID specifically for camera traffic, prioritize camera QoS to prevent stream stuttering, and establish automated P2P WireGuard mesh tunnels across the property.

Why is the application distributed outside Google Play & Apple App Store? ↓

Commercial app stores aggressively kill background camera services, restrict persistent battery-saver bypasses, and take 30% revenue cuts. By distributing exclusively via F-Droid, GitHub Releases, and Patreon, our software maintains 24/7 background execution rights and full device control.
